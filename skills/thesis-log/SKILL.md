# Thesis log and outline

## Thesis versions

A thesis statement earns its keep by being falsifiable, specific about the
target, and clear about the contribution. Weak versions describe a topic; strong
versions stake a claim someone could resist.

Work in `00-Admin/thesis-log.md`. Each version gets a dated entry with the
statement in full and a note on what changed and why.

When proposing a revision, write it as a suggestion block:

```markdown
### Suggestion (date)
Proposed: (the revised statement)
Why: (what this fixes: scope, falsifiability, the contribution, the target)
Cost: (what it commits the author to proving)
```

Never overwrite the author's own entries. They accept, reject or rewrite.

Test each candidate against four questions and report the answers:

1. Could a competent person disagree with this? If not, it is a topic.
2. What exactly must be proved, and which chapter proves it?
3. What is the contribution: critique, construction, or both?
4. Does the scope match the word limit?

## Outline

Build `40-Outline/outline.md` as a table, one row per section:

| Section | Claim to establish | Moves and sources | Word budget |

Rules for a workable outline:

- Budgets sum to the word limit, with about 5 per cent held back.
- Every chapter has one job, stateable in a sentence.
- Sources named per section come only from notes at `read` or `verified`.
- The chapter that carries the central argument gets the largest budget, and
  the chapters supplying its premises come before it.
- An objections chapter is worth its space: it is where a marker looks to see
  whether the author has understood the opposition.

Revisit budgets when the thesis changes. Report drift between planned and
actual counts whenever chapters are written.
